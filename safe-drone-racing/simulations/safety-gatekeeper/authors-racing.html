What is being checked?

MPPI chooses velocity commands using the base model plus learned acceleration residual. Each candidate follows those commands, then a slower course-following velocity schedule. The same neural feedback controller produces thrust and torque during prediction, verification and simulation.

The model-error bound is evaluated over each velocity region. Uncertainty is injected into acceleration and propagated through all 12 states.

Model, assumptions and limits

The controller has the DiffReach authors’ architecture with locally trained weights. The matching 12-state plant uses position, velocity, Euler angles and body angular rates, with thrust and roll/pitch torque commands. It is a different model from the existing 14-state racing replay.

Initial state is exact. Position inputs to the network use a fixed origin at the start of the candidate. Velocity commands and that origin stay identical during verification and execution. The backup schedule is generated using predicted course-following motion; it is then followed by the neural controller.

The residual is learned from exact acceleration data. Its global smoothness bounds are known in this controlled experiment. Stronger residual cases scale the simulated residual, learned model and error bounds together.

These are finite-horizon numerical enclosures. Floating-point rounding is not formally certified, and no terminal invariant-set or indefinite backup-safety proof has been established. The zero-switch startup is available under the stated startup assumption even if its numerical check fails; later candidates get no such override. No closed-loop racing episode or online retraining is claimed by this page.
