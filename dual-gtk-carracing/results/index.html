What the reported outcomes mean

Executed violation: the simulated car violates a state or tire-force constraint during closed-loop driving. The simulator stops at that violation; it does not simulate the resulting collision or loss of control.

Candidate rollout failure: a fixed candidate prefix followed by fallback fails a state constraint, tire-force constraint, or terminal recovery condition for one sampled friction value. This is the event counted in certification and independent candidate tests.

Candidate tolerance ε: the maximum accepted upper confidence bound on candidate failure probability. The 1%, 5% and 10% settings are thresholds for individual candidate checks, not target violation rates for driving episodes.

How the probabilistic acceptance rule permits a violation

Matched example: true friction μ = 0.45, initial uncertainty [0.45, 1.05], seed 20100. At 0.30 s, the three tolerance settings generate identical informative controls and reference trajectories. A 0.3 s informative prefix has a 4.081% upper failure bound: 1% rejects it, while 5% and 10% accept it. A 1.2 s prefix has a 9.447% bound and passes only at 10%.

The 1% controller selects a nominal prefix and completes eight seconds without a violation. The 5% and 10% controllers select informative prefixes and violate the tire-force constraint at 0.50 s. The realized friction lies in the unsafe subset permitted by their probabilistic certificates.

Separate candidate test: 10,000 fresh friction draws per selected policy produce 0, 230 and 530 failed prefix-plus-fallback rollouts. The corresponding 0%, 2.30% and 5.30% are observed candidate failure rates, not whole-episode crash rates.

Across 214 exploratory executions, 18 contain tire-force violations. All 214 replay audits reproduce the recorded outcomes correctly, including those 18 violations. MPPI, SMID, selection and DRS logic are unchanged. Targeted case counts do not estimate general driving failure probabilities.
