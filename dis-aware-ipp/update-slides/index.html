Method proposal for review, 20 October 2026. The first contribution is disturbance-aware prediction of delivered monitoring. The revision introduces optimism over performance outcomes and importance-corrected proposal adaptation. It holds the posterior fixed inside predictions and learns from real measurements. The new method has not been implemented or evaluated. All previous value-of-learning equations and their associated results are retained in the final Archived method section.

An informative plan can lose its value in execution

The central issue is that a nominally informative trajectory may not deliver its predicted monitoring performance when executed under uncertain disturbances.

1. Wind-aware planning
Predict how wind and controller intervention change motion, arrival time and sensing.
2. Optimistic planning with online learning
Try favorable plausible alternatives and learn from actual execution.

The paths are conceptual illustrations on the recorded A/B geometry, not measured comparisons or a new experiment. Wind arrows use the archived physical field for explanation. The planner only has its estimated wind belief. The first question is whether the robot can deliver the sensing predicted by a nominal route. The second is whether optimism encourages useful early experience that improves later monitoring. This is a hypothesis for the revised method. Uncertainty reduction is not the task objective.

Method assessment before implementation

The specification is mathematically coherent: a defined optimistic target, a state-dependent execution model and an importance-corrected proposal.

The intended payoff is conditional: an early experiment can improve later monitoring when its observations change useful future decisions. The optimistic criterion does not calculate that payoff.

Important failure modes: favorable-tail sampling error, poorly calibrated wind beliefs, repeated optimism about uninformative regions, inadequate route representation and excessive computation time.

The next stage is to test the hypothesis. Existing simulations provide no evidence for this revised algorithm.
