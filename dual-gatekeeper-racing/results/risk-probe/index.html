Observed probabilistic failures

Low grip exposes the risk that a probabilistic certificate permits. These experiments change only scenario parameters: friction, starting position and candidate tolerance. The MPPI optimizer, fixed informative γ = 2, SMID update, selection rule and DRS test are unchanged.

A reproducible failure

Start at 0 m, seed 20100, true friction μ = 0.45, initial support [0.45, 1.05]. At 0.30 s, measurements have reduced the support to [0.45, 0.578775]. The 5% and 10% controllers accept informative prefixes containing a small set of unsafe friction values. The realized friction lies in that set: both violate the tire-force constraint at 0.50 s. The 1% controller chooses a nominal prefix and completes eight seconds safely.

This is a modeled combined tire-force constraint violation. The simulator stops at the violation; it does not simulate a collision.

The tolerance changes acceptance

The informative controls, reference states and certification sample seeds at 0.30 s are identical across tolerances. Only the risk threshold changes which prefixes pass. This isolates the probabilistic acceptance effect from optimizer randomness.

Rejection can stop sampling early. The displayed full-batch bounds come from the matching completed 1,000-sample checks at the higher tolerances.

The certificate permits a small unsafe subset of the current friction support. A realized friction value in that subset can violate the constraint even though the plan passes its probabilistic check. The observed candidate failure rates remain below the allowed tolerances.

Nearby friction and 10% comparison

At the same seed, the 5% runs fail at the tested values from 0.4501 through 0.4525; the 10% runs also fail at 0.453, 0.4535, 0.454 and 0.455. All ten matched 1% runs complete safely. This demonstrates failure at interior friction values as well as the support endpoint.

All 64 runs at 10% using the original [0.76, 1.05] distribution stayed safe. Increasing tolerance permits more risk but does not force a failure in every operating condition.
